# Conclusion and limitations

## Conclusion: answer the research question

State what the work establishes, connect the contribution to the original problem, and give the supported implication. Keep this shorter than the introduction. Avoid repeating a table of gains, introducing new evidence, broadening the claim, or appending generic future-work promises.

A useful future direction follows from a specific unresolved condition and is clearly prospective. It is optional unless required by the requested format.

## Limitations: state concrete boundaries

Identify an assumption, observed failure, evaluation restriction, or resource requirement and explain its consequence for use or interpretation. Keep limitations specific to the research. Distinguish a known failure from an untested setting; lack of testing does not establish failure.

State critical boundaries where the relevant claim appears, even if a separate limitations section is required. Use the venue's required placement and page accounting when known. Avoid repeated “we do not claim” disclaimers and speculative lists of everything not studied. Preserve material negative findings and required ethical or responsible-research disclosures.
