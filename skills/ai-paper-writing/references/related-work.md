# Related work

Position the contribution among ideas, assumptions, and available information. Organize paragraphs around meaningful methodological relationships, not author-by-author summaries or a chronological bibliography.

For each relevant group, explain the shared approach, what it accomplishes, and the distinction that matters to this paper. Give the closest work enough detail to make the novelty comparison credible. Compare like settings: supervision, model access, training data, inference budget, or evaluation task may explain apparent differences.

Use established terminology from the community and preserve distinctions between neighboring concepts. Support claims about prior work with the paper itself or authoritative records. Do not invent a research category simply to isolate the proposed method, and do not claim no prior work exists without adequate retrieval.

Place related work early when readers need it to understand the problem or method; later placement can work when the introduction already establishes the closest comparison. Detailed placement is a narrative decision unless the venue specifies it. Avoid repeating the full survey in the introduction. The introduction establishes the decisive gap; this section supplies broader context and precise comparison.
