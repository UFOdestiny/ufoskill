# Title and abstract

## Title: identify the task and contribution

Use familiar terms that let the intended community recognize the problem and technical idea. Prefer a specific, readable description over a chain of coined concepts. A method name is optional; preserve an established name if supplied. Avoid an acronym-only title, unsupported superiority, and a title whose scope exceeds the evaluation.

## Abstract: give a complete miniature argument

Cover the concrete problem, specific gap, key insight, what the method or study does, the central supported result, and the implication. These are logical functions, not a fixed sentence count. Use one paragraph unless the venue requires another format; follow its word limit.

Explain the contribution before presenting a benchmark gain. Include a result or comparison when it makes the claim assessable; avoid a list of datasets and numbers that displaces the idea. Specify the comparison or setting needed to interpret a numerical result. Include a consequential boundary if omitting it would make the takeaway misleading.

Use few abbreviations and no notation that requires the main text to understand. Make the abstract understandable on its own. It should not promise a theorem, robustness result, or efficiency improvement absent from the paper. Align its strongest claim with the experiment or analysis that supports it.
