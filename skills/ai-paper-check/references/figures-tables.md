# Figures and tables

Inspect each figure and table within the requested scope, using the source values and rendered artifact when available. Apply the [evidence checks](evidence-citations.md) to numerical claims and the [notation checks](notation-abbreviations.md) to symbols and abbreviations. If only source text or an image is supplied, report which aspects could not be verified.

## Identity, interpretation, and evidence

1. Verify that the figure or table is referenced in the text and that each reference points to the intended object, panel, row, or column. Check numbering and labels across the main text and appendix.
2. Make the caption sufficiently self-contained to identify the task, compared methods or conditions, reported quantity, and essential interpretation rules. Link detailed protocol rather than repeating it in every caption.
3. Define axes, units, tick scales, legends, panel labels, symbols, and abbreviations. Identify logarithmic axes, normalization, truncated ranges, or differing panel scales when these affect interpretation.
4. Explain higher-is-better and lower-is-better metrics, aggregation, sample or run counts, and error bars or uncertainty intervals. Distinguish standard deviation, standard error, and confidence intervals when applicable.
5. Explain missing values, dashes, unavailable runs, and significance markers. Distinguish zero from missing data. Verify that a significance claim has a corresponding test, comparison, and reported criterion.
6. Match dataset, model, method, metric, split, and setting names to the experimental protocol. Make different budgets, supervision, or oracle access visible when they affect comparability.
7. Trace important cells and plotted points to supplied results where possible. Recompute headline differences and aggregates when source values permit, preserving rounding, units, denominators, and the distinction between relative gains and percentage points.
8. Check that the prose describes the actual trend, including meaningful exceptions. A caption or plot must not convert correlation into a causal conclusion or a selected setting into a universal gain.

## Highlighting and presentation

1. Follow the manuscript's stated highlighting convention and explain it in the caption or note. If best and second-best results are marked, verify both ranks against metric direction and the eligible comparison group.
2. Handle ties and rounding consistently. Do not automatically highlight the proposed method or rank incompatible settings together. Separate non-comparable oracle results when appropriate.
3. Inspect rendered boldface, underlining, colors, markers, and line styles for agreement with the intended meaning. Check legibility at the final paper scale, clipped labels, overlapping text, and content outside the page or column.
4. Verify that captions, legends, and surrounding prose identify the same curves, panels, or table groups. Check framework diagrams against the described inputs, operations, connections, and outputs.
5. Identify the claim or explanatory purpose served by the object. Flag redundant objects or secondary detail that could move to the appendix; keep evidence central to a contribution accessible in the main text. Recommend substantive redesign through the review workflow when needed.

Use the shared finding format, identifying the object and panel or cell, source value or visual evidence, and effect on interpretation. A clean build does not establish visual correctness, and agreement among manuscript tables does not verify their underlying measurements.
